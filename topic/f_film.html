Introduction

More Than Entertainment

Film is a powerful medium that reflects culture, history, and human experiences through visual storytelling. By combining images, sound, and narrative, films communicate ideas and emotions that influence how audiences understand society and reality. In the Philippines, cinema has played a vital role in shaping national identity — from early filmmaking to contemporary and independent productions.

Key Terms

Core Concepts

Film
A form of visual art that uses moving images and sound to tell stories, express ideas, and reflect culture, history, and human experiences.
Cinema
The art, industry, and cultural practice of making and exhibiting films — also films as a collective body of artistic and social expression.
Philippine Cinema
Films produced in the Philippines reflecting Filipino culture, values, social issues, and historical experiences, from early to contemporary productions.
Film Analysis
The critical examination of a film's narrative, visual elements, sound, and themes to understand how meaning is created.
Film Criticism
The evaluation and interpretation of films using established theoretical approaches.
Symbolism
The use of objects, colors, actions, sounds, or images to represent deeper meaning — expressing ideas not directly stated.

Philippine Cinema

A Living Legacy

Philippine cinema began in 1897, making it one of the earliest film industries in Asia. It reached its Golden Age in the 1950s–60s, then grew through Martial Law resistance, and continues to shine on the international stage.

Classic Filipino Films

Known for its studio system (LVN, Sampaguita, Premiere), literary adaptations, and the emergence of directorial authors. Melodrama, musicals, and black-and-white masterwork cinematography defined the era.

Film Analysis

Formal Elements

Film analysis transforms the viewer from a passive audience into an active analyst. By dissecting cinematography, editing, and sound design, you see how film is designed to influence emotion and mind.

🎥
Cinematography
How a film is captured using the camera — angles, shots, and movements.
🎭
Mise-en-Scène
Everything visible in a scene — setting, props, and actors — creating mood and telling the story visually.
✂️
Editing
How shots are arranged together. Controls the flow and pace — fast, slow, exciting, or emotional.
🔊
Sound
Dialogue, music, and effects. Adds emotion, builds atmosphere, and helps explain the story.
💡
Lighting
Bright lighting feels positive; dark lighting creates mystery or tension.
👗
Costume & Makeup
Show a character's personality, status, and time period — making characters realistic and believable.
🎬
Acting
Characters portrayed through expressions, voice, and actions — making the story believable and engaging.
Levels of Analysis

Ways of Reading Film

1
Text-Based Film Analysis
Examines a film's narrative structure, plot, characters, and visual storytelling to understand how these elements reveal themes, motifs, and overall meaning.
2
Semiotic Analysis
Examines the signs and symbols in a film — visual, auditory, and linguistic — to understand how they create meaning and influence audience interpretation.
3
Topic-Based / Narrative Approach
Focuses on a film's themes, ideas, and messages, examining how narrative elements like plot and characters convey deeper social or philosophical issues.
4
Picture and Sound / Iconic Analysis
Examines visual and audio elements — composition, lighting, camera work, editing, and sound — to understand how they shape storytelling, mood, and symbolic meaning.
5
Psychoanalytical Approach
Draws on Freudian theories to explore unconscious elements — characters' motivations, psychological dynamics, desires, fears, and conflicts.
6
Cultural and Historical Approach
Examines a film within its cultural and historical milieu — analyzing how it reflects and responds to social, political, and cultural realities of its time.
Story Structure

Three Act Structure

I
Setup
Introduces the characters, setting, and story world. Establishes the world the audience will inhabit.
II
Rising Action
Problems and challenges arise, creating tension and excitement as the main character struggles to achieve their goal.
III
Resolution
Concludes the story by resolving the conflict and showing the final outcome, giving audiences a sense of closure.
Film Criticism

Critical Approaches

01 — FORMALISM
Formalist Approach
Treats film as an expressive artistic medium rather than a documentary one. Focuses on camera angles, editing, visual style, sound and music, and story structure as the primary vehicles of meaning.
02 — AUTEUR
Auteur Theory
Proposes that the director is the true author of a film. Originated with François Truffaut in the 1950s. An auteur demonstrates technical competence, signature style, and recurring themes across their body of work.
"Even though filmmaking is collaborative, the director's creative identity is what defines the work."
03 — HISTORICAL
Historical Approach
Considers the historical context in which a film was made. Explores production history, reception history, genre evolution, and national cinema as expressions of their era.
Key Question: What does the film reveal about the era it was created in?
04 — FEMINISM
Feminist Approach
Examines cinema through gender, power, and representation. Laura Mulvey's "male gaze" (1975) argues mainstream cinema is structured around a masculine viewpoint. Incorporates race, class, and sexuality through intersectionality.
05 — SOCIOLOGICAL
Sociological Approach
Examines film as a reflection of society — class, race, power, and cultural norms. Essential for Philippine cinema's socially conscious films on poverty, corruption, and marginalized struggles.
Example: Maynila sa Kuko ng Liwanag (1975) — exposing urban poverty and systemic exploitation under Martial Law.
06 — MYTHOLOGICAL
Mythological Approach
Interprets films through universal symbols, archetypes, and recurring narrative patterns rooted in Carl Jung and Joseph Campbell. Identifies the Hero's Journey and archetypal characters.
Example: Heneral Luna (2015) — the Tragic Hero, Mentor (Mabini), Loyal Companions, and Shadow figures.
07 — MARXISM
Marxist Approach
Analyzes cinema through class struggle, ideology, and economic power. Core concepts include base/superstructure, ideology, hegemony, the culture industry, class struggle, and false consciousness.
Films contribute to hegemony by presenting the perspectives of the powerful as universal truths.
Filipino films can touch the hearts of Filipino audiences in ways that foreign movies cannot — they reinforce cultural values such as family, resilience, and social relationships, influencing how audiences perceive their identity.
— Montes (2015) & Capul et al. (2025)
Conclusion

Beyond the Screen

Philippine cinema is not only entertainment — it is a meaningful reflection of Filipino identity, emotions, and social realities. Films have the power to shape how people think and understand society.

Films communicate ideas using narrative, symbolism, and technical elements — each choice crafted to influence emotion and perspective.
Philippine films promote culture, Filipino values, and social awareness — shaping national identity across generations.
Different approaches and ethical theories (utilitarianism, deontology, virtue ethics) help in interpreting the moral dimensions of films.
Are films simply for enjoyment, or tools for awareness and reflection? Viewers are encouraged to think critically and engage with the deeper messages behind every film.
